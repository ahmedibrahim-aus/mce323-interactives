What to notice

  1. Stress goes as 1/d³. Drag the diameter and watch the curve — a 10% larger shaft buys about 33% more factor of safety. That cubic is the single most useful number in shaft design.
  2. Two checks, and the lower one wins. Fatigue usually governs, but the yield check is not optional. Scaling both loads together will never change which one governs — that is set by the Ma:Tm ratio and the strengths. Try Torque-dominated and watch the two margins close.
  3. Look at the split. The bar shows how much of the required d³ comes from the alternating bending term A/Se versus the steady torsion term B/Sut, both in mm³. In most rotating shafts bending dominates — but a mixer or a low-speed drive is legitimately torsion-dominated, as the Torque-dominated preset shows.
  4. Round up, then re-check. Solve for d and you get a decimal. This rounds to the next whole millimetre; a real specification would go to the next preferred size (R20: 25, 28, 30, 32, 35, 40 …) and re-confirm n.
