What to notice

  1. Principal planes are the planes of zero shear. Sweep θ and stop where the shear curve crosses zero — σx′ is simultaneously at an extreme. That coincidence is the definition, not a consequence of it.
  2. σx′ + σy′ never changes. Watch the invariant while everything else moves. It is the reason the centre of Mohr's circle sits still.
  3. Maximum shear is always 45° from the principal planes — for every state you can type in. And there, σx′ = σy′ = σavg.
  4. Mohr's circle turns twice as fast. Rotate the element θ and the point travels 2θ. Try Pure torsion: principal planes land at 45°, which is why a twisted piece of chalk breaks on a 45° helix.
