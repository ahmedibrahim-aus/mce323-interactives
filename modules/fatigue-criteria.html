What to notice

  1. Mean stress is what hurts. Set σa = 100 and slide σm from 0 outward. The amplitude never changes, yet every factor of safety falls. A part cycling 0→200 MPa is in more danger than one cycling −100→+100 MPa.
  2. The load line matters, not just the point. Press Push out: the point travels along a fixed R-ratio and the safety factor is the ratio of where it crosses the envelope to where it sits now.
  3. Two failure modes, not one. The Langer line is first-cycle yielding. At high mean stress it cuts in front of every fatigue criterion — the part yields before it ever gets the chance to fatigue.
  4. The criteria fan out to the right. Near σm = 0 they agree. Push σm toward Sut and Soderberg is far below Gerber. Choosing between them is a real design decision, not bookkeeping.
