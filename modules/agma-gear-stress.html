What to notice

  1. Set every factor to 1 and the method is one line: σ = Wt / (F·mt·J). Everything else is a correction. Students who see this first stop being frightened of the equation.
  2. The corrections are not equal. Switch them on one at a time and watch the waterfall — Kv and Ks usually move the number far more than KB. Knowing which ones matter is engineering judgement.
  3. You cannot compare SF with SH directly. Bending stress is proportional to load; contact stress goes as its square root. So the honest comparison is SF against SH², and it usually changes the answer.
  4. Wear normally governs a well-made gearset. Raise the hardness and watch the wear factor climb much faster than the bending factor — which is why gear teeth are surface hardened.
