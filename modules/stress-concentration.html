What to notice

  1. Read the chart in four steps. Work out the ratio the axis wants, find the right curve, read across, then sanity-check: Kt is never below 1, and a sharper notch always gives a higher Kt. If your answer moved the wrong way, you read the wrong curve.
  2. Ignoring Kt does not make a part safer, it makes your estimate wrong. Watch the two factors of safety separate. For a plate with a hole it is routinely a factor of two and a half.
  3. Sharp notches are expensive. Drag r toward zero and Kt climbs without limit. Almost all of the benefit of a generous fillet arrives in the first millimetre or two.
  4. Fatigue does not feel the full Kt. Notch sensitivity q pulls it back to Kf = 1 + q(Kt − 1). Small radii and softer steels are less sensitive — which is why a mild steel tolerates a notch better than a hardened one.
